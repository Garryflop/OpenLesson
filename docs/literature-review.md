# Literature review

13 sources reviewed (required: at least 12), of them 9 scientific articles in Scopus Q1-Q3 journals (required: at least 8).

**Search and selection:** peer-reviewed journals and conference papers, 2020-2026, teachers' professional
development, free full text only. Keywords: peer observation of teaching, online teacher professional development, MOOC, learning platform.

## Themes

| Theme [sources] | What the literature agrees on | What is missing | Decision for OpenLesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Peer observation of teaching [1-2] | Developmental, collegial observation is valued. Feedback needs strengths, weaknesses and comments | Digital support for the whole cycle. Only 3 of 13 observation tools are validated | Status-based cycle, configurable rubric, teacher reply (M1-M3, M6) |
| Online PD platforms and their design [3-8] | Dashboards and history support growth. Platform quality, usability and accessibility drive satisfaction. Moodle-type platforms suit university staff | Platforms train teachers, none runs open lessons and peer observation | Teacher cabinet and dashboard, SUS at least 70, WCAG 2.1 AA (M8) |
| Effect and uptake of online PD and MOOCs [9-13] | Online courses and MOOCs raise teachers' digital competences when they fit their needs. Usefulness, ease of use and time decide uptake | Acceptance data for peer-observation tools | Reminders 10 / 7 / 3 days, UTAUT survey in the pilot (M2) |

**Evidence base:** 13 sources, 2020-2026: 2 reviews, 10 empirical and design studies, 1 conceptual paper.

**Research gap:** every building block is studied separately. No source joins scheduling, deadline control,
attendance, peer feedback and AI summaries for open lessons at a university. OpenLesson fills this gap.
